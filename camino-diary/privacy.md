# Camino Diary privacy policy

Camino Diary is a trip planner and diary for the Camino de Santiago, made by Seanchas (TODO: legal name as registered
in the Play Console). This policy says what the app does with your information.

**In short: your plans, diary pages, photos and stamps stay on your phone. We don't collect them, and we can't see
them.** There is no account and no sign-in.

## What the app keeps, and where

Everything you put into Camino Diary is stored on your phone, inside the app's own storage:

- your trips, stages and plans;
- your diary pages;
- the photos and stamp images you add (the app keeps its own copy of each);
- your settings.

None of it is sent to us. The app has no servers of its own.

**Maps.** The routes, their stages and places come with the app. The offline map of a route is a download that the app
asks for when you tap Download, or leave the box ticked when you start a trip. It comes through Google Play, the way
the app itself did, so Google sees it as it sees any Play download, under its own privacy policy; we don't receive
anything about it beyond the counts described under Crash reports and usage counts below. Once a map is on your phone,
looking at it sends nothing anywhere.

**Photos.** You choose photos through Android's photo picker, which gives the app only the photos you pick. The
picker removes the location from those photos. A photo taken with the Camera button in the app is taken by your
camera app, and may keep the location your camera app records. Camino Diary does not read photo locations.

**Where you are.** If you tap Show where I am on Today, the app asks for your location, and from then on uses it to
show you on the day's map and say how far you've come and how far there is to go. It reads your location only while
Today or a day's map is open, never in the background, and keeps it in memory only: it is never saved, never added to
your diary, and never sent anywhere. Approximate location is enough. You can take the permission back in your phone's
settings at any time. (On Android 11 and older, finding a buddy's phone also counts as location; see Sending a plan
below.)

## Copies you choose to make

Your data only leaves the app when you, or Android at your request, copy it somewhere:

- **Android backup.** If backup is on for your phone, Android copies your trips, pages and settings (not your photos)
  to your Google account, and can copy everything, photos included, when you move to a new phone. Google handles this
  under your Google account and its terms; we can't see it. You can turn it off in your phone's settings.
- **Automatic copy.** If you turn it on in Settings, the app saves a copy of each trip, photos included, into a folder
  you choose. If that folder is in a cloud service such as Google Drive, that service's terms apply to it.
- **Export.** When you export a trip, the app makes a file and you choose where it goes or who it's shared with.
- **Sharing a day.** When you tap Share on a written day, the app makes one picture of it (its photo or map, its
  route and distance, and your words only if you switch them in) and hands it to the app you choose, such as a chat
  app. It goes nowhere else.
- **Sending a plan.** When you send a trip's plan to a walking buddy, it goes straight to their phone: as a file you
  share (for example by Quick Share, a chat app or email), or phone to phone: their camera reads the code on your
  screen. It never passes through us. A plan holds the trip's days and the name you give; before sending you choose
  what goes with them (each kind of stop, other ways and day notes), and your bookings go only if you switch them in.
  Your diary pages and photos are never sent. Receiving over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi shows the sender your phone's name,
  as set in Android's settings (or its model, if it has no name).

  The code on your screen holds the plan itself (never your bookings), so a buddy takes it straight from their
  camera, with no connection. Anyone who scans the code while it shows can read the plan.

  To read the code, the app asks for the camera when you open Receive a plan. The app uses it only while that screen
  is open, to look for the code; no picture or video is recorded or kept, and nothing the camera sees leaves your
  phone.

  When a plan is too big for the code, or goes with your bookings, the phones find each other over Bluetooth and
  Wi-Fi. For that, Android asks for the "nearby devices" permission; on Android 11 and older it calls this location.
  The app uses it only to find the other phone while Send this plan or Receive a plan is open, and never to work out
  or record where you are.

  Reading the code uses Google's ML Kit, which runs on your phone; finding the other phone uses Nearby Connections
  from Google Play services. Both may send Google diagnostics such as your phone's model, Android version, country,
  and how well they worked, under Google's own privacy policy; we don't receive them. For Google Play services, you
  can turn this off in your phone's settings, under Google, then Usage & diagnostics.

## Crash reports and usage counts

If something goes wrong, or when you use a part of the app, Camino Diary can send a short report to Sentry (Functional
Software, Inc., United States), which we use to find and fix problems and to see which parts of the app are used.

- **A crash report** holds the error, your phone's model and Android version, the app's version, and a random ID the
  app made for itself. It never holds your pages, photos, stamps, places, trip names, plans or your location, and not
  your name, email address or IP address.
- **A usage count** says that something happened (a page was saved, a photo was added, a day was split, a trip was
  started, a route's map was downloaded, how long a download took or whether it failed), with sizes in rough ranges
  and, for routes, which of the app's own Caminos it was (for example the Camino Francés). It never holds anything
  you wrote or where you are.

Reports are kept for 30 days. You can switch both off in Settings, under Diagnostics; the app then sends nothing at
all. To have the reports from your phone deleted, email us the ID shown there.

## Contact

If you email us at seanchas.apps@gmail.com (for example with Send feedback in Settings), we receive your email address and what you write. We
use them only to reply, and we don't add you to any list.

## What we don't do

- No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
- No selling or sharing of your information.
- No tracking across other apps or websites.

## Deleting your data

Deleting a trip in the app deletes its pages, photos and stamps. Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on
your phone. Copies you made yourself (exports, the automatic copy's folder) and your Android backup are yours to
delete; your Google account settings show and delete the backup.

## Children

Camino Diary is not directed at children. It collects no information from anyone, children included.

## Your rights

Because we don't hold any of your information, there is normally nothing for us to give you, correct or delete. If
you've emailed us and want that email deleted, ask and we will delete it. If you're in the EU or the UK, you also have
the right to complain to your data protection authority.
